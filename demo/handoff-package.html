What This Is

WindfallRouter demonstrates that inference routing is the first real-world subjective decision that needs onchain consensus — and GenLayer is the only chain that can do it.

When an AI gateway routes a query, it balances latency, model quality, and carbon impact. No formula resolves this tradeoff — it's inherently subjective. Today, one server decides and you trust its word. WindfallRouter puts that decision to 5 validators who independently reason about whether the choice is defensible.

The demo should make GenLayer the hero. The judges should walk away thinking "inference routing is a killer app for our platform."

Key Talking Points

The narrative frame: Inference routing is the first real-world subjective decision that needs onchain consensus — and GenLayer is the only chain that can do it. But we go further: the verification layer has the same carbon problem as the inference layer, and we're the only team with the energy data to solve both.
  • Defensible, not identical. Validators don't need to pick the same node. They check whether the leader's reasoning is coherent. Different LLMs, different reasoning paths, same conclusion — that's Optimistic Democracy working.
  • The hard case is the interesting case. "Smartest" obviously routes to the best model. "Greenest" obviously routes to the cleanest node. "Balanced" — that's where the subjectivity lives, where validators might reason differently, and where GenLayer does real work.
  • The verification paradox. Proving inference was green costs 5 more LLM calls (validators). Nobody tracks the carbon of consensus. GenLayer is the only chain where this matters (no other chain runs LLM inference for validation), and we have the energy data to measure it.

Video Script — 2 Minutes

Before recording: Do 2–3 test routings first to confirm the testnet is responsive. If routing takes >30 seconds, cut during the spinner and resume when the result appears. Pre-load the History tab so it has entries.

Setup Checklist

  • Open frontend-ecofrontiers.vercel.app — green "Bradbury Testnet" badge visible top-right
  • Open explorer-bradbury.genlayer.com in another tab
  • QuickTime or OBS, 1920×1080
  • Do a test routing to confirm it works
  • Check History tab has 3+ entries
  • Hide bookmarks bar (Cmd+Shift+B)
Beat 1 — The Problem brief
[Screen: WindfallRouter frontend. Briefly show the three node cards.]
"AI inference routing is a subjective decision. Fast or green? Quality or cheap? There's no formula. Today, one server decides and you trust its word. WindfallRouter puts that decision to a jury."
Beat 2 — The Hard Case ~35 seconds
[Click the "Balanced" preset. Sliders move to Latency 5, Quality 5, Carbon 5.]
"Three nodes. Finland is the greenest. Virginia is the fastest and smartest. Germany is in between. I set all three priorities equal — latency, quality, carbon. There's no right answer here. This is the genuinely subjective call."
[Type in prompt box: "Compare the environmental cost of training GPT-4 versus fine-tuning an open model"
Click "Route Inference". Wait for result.]
"Five validators on GenLayer's Bradbury testnet independently reason about this. The leader proposes a routing decision. Each validator checks: is this choice defensible given the priorities?"
[Result appears with validator vote circles.]
"The validators might use different LLMs. They might reason differently about the tradeoffs. But they converge on whether the leader's answer is defensible — not whether it's the only possible answer. That's subjective consensus."
Beat 3 — The Easy Case ~20 seconds
[Click the "Greenest" preset. Sliders move to Latency 0, Quality 0, Carbon 10.]
[Type: "What is the carbon footprint of AI inference?"
Click "Route Inference". Wait for result.]
"Now compare: carbon is the only priority. Finland, 45 grams CO2 — ten times cleaner than Virginia. When the answer is obvious, consensus is trivial. When it's subjective, Optimistic Democracy does real work. That's the whole point."
Beat 4 — Onchain Verification ~15 seconds
[Point to validator vote circles. Then click "View on Explorer".]
"Every routing decision is onchain. The validator votes, the reasoning, the chosen node — all verifiable on the Bradbury block explorer. No trust required."
Beat 5 — Who Watches the Watchmen ~25 seconds
[Switch back to the frontend. Hold on the map showing the three nodes.]
"But here's what nobody's asking. Every GenLayer transaction runs five LLM inference calls — one per validator. That's five times the compute of the original request. As GenLayer scales to thousands of validators, the carbon footprint of consensus itself becomes a real problem."
"WindfallRouter's energy data doesn't just route inference — it can route verification. Carbon-aware validator selection. End-to-end carbon transparency, from the query to the consensus that verified it. That's what we're building next on GenLayer."

Recording Tips

  • Total target: 1:40 – 2:00
  • Pacing: Beat 2 (the hard case) is the centerpiece. Take your time there. Beat 5 (ecosystem) should feel like you're opening a door, not rushing to close.
  • Mouse: Move slowly and deliberately. Hover on things you're talking about.
  • Voiceover: Record separately and sync in iMovie, or record live. Either works.
  • If routing takes >30s: Cut during the "Validators reasoning..." spinner. Resume when result appears.
  • If a routing fails: Try again. Testnet can be flaky. Two successful routings is all we need.
  • Resolution: 1920×1080. Chrome, clean tab bar.

Ecosystem Extensions (for judges / Q&A)

If asked "where does this go?" — the core thesis is the verification layer has the same carbon problem as the inference layer, and we have the data infrastructure to solve both.

The Verification Paradox

Proving your inference was green requires 5 validator LLM calls. Each validator is a machine on some grid, consuming energy that nobody tracks. As GenLayer scales from 5 to 1,000 validators, the carbon footprint of consensus becomes significant. No other chain has this problem because no other chain runs LLM inference as part of consensus.

Carbon-tagged validatorsEach GenLayer validator reports its grid zone. GridOracle extends to cover validator locations, not just inference nodes.GridOracle already deployed with zone-level carbon data. Same infrastructure, new consumer.
Carbon cost of consensusEvery WindfallRouter transaction reports two numbers: carbon of the routed inference AND carbon of the 5 validators who verified it. End-to-end transparency.We already calculate per-request energy cost for inference nodes. Apply same math to validators.
